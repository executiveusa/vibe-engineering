# Upstream inspiration and attribution

Vibe Engineering is an independent open-source method. Some skills in this repository were re-authored after studying other MIT-licensed open-source projects. We keep useful engineering ideas, rename and adapt them to Vibe doctrine, and do not present upstream authors' work as our own original invention.

## blader/humanizer

Source: https://github.com/blader/humanizer
License: MIT

Vibe adaptations: `human-voice` and parts of `stop-slop`.

Carried forward: preserve factual meaning, remove robotic AI-writing habits, avoid invented details, prefer normal human language, and review formatting as well as word choice.

Changed for Vibe: writing quality is part of a broader anti-slop check that also covers product clarity, design defaults, proof, accessibility, integration, and ownership.

## Leonxlnx/unlazy

Source: https://github.com/Leonxlnx/unlazy
License: MIT

Vibe adaptation: `deep-work`.

Carried forward: define completion gates before large work, decompose large tasks, make incomplete work visible, and independently re-verify before claiming completion.

Changed for Vibe: the pattern is folded into Intent / Standard / Evidence and Vibe release governance rather than copying Unlazy's implementation or security machinery.

## mattpocock/skills

Source: https://github.com/mattpocock/skills
License: MIT

Vibe studied the promoted engineering and productivity skills and re-authored reusable patterns with Vibe-native names and contracts. The adapted areas include interview-driven alignment, shared project language, ADR discipline, specs, tracer-bullet tickets, test-first development, debugging, architecture review, code review, handoffs, human-only setup steps, and skill routing.

The Vibe catalog is not a mirror. Vibe adds its own rules: verify first, stop slop, preserve owner control, use independent review, prove the exact revision, and keep rollback before release.
