# Heart & Soul Design Standard

Layer 3 stable design doctrine for Vibe Engineering and ICM-governed products.

## Purpose

Design is not decoration added after engineering. It is the deliberate shaping of human attention, effort, confidence, emotion, and control around a real outcome.

The governing principle is subtraction:

> Keep removing until another removal would harm user success, comprehension, trust, accessibility, control, emotional resonance, or essential product identity.

Minimalism is optional. Essentiality is mandatory.

## Heart

Before choosing interface, style, motion, component, or feature, identify:

- the human outcome;
- the reason it matters now;
- the primary journey;
- the desired feeling;
- the trust the user needs;
- the control the user must retain.

If the primary job cannot be stated plainly, the design is not ready to expand.

## Soul

A strong experience feels intentional rather than generated from defaults. It should create coherent psychological momentum through hierarchy, contrast, rhythm, spacing, type, motion, language, state, and restraint.

The goal is not spectacle. The goal is an experience whose important parts feel inevitable.

A design may be quiet or bold, sparse or expressive. It must still make the next important thing legible and make unnecessary things disappear.

## Invisible complexity

Power may increase behind the interface without increasing visible burden.

Prefer:

`system detects → system prepares → human reviews when consequence requires it`

over:

`human configures → human manages → human repairs avoidable complexity`

Do not expose implementation complexity merely because the system contains it.

## Human control

Automation removes unnecessary work, not accountability.

Require review or explicit confirmation when actions are consequential, ambiguous, difficult to reverse, externally visible, financially meaningful, privacy-sensitive, or likely to benefit from human judgment.

## Seven design lenses

Use these lenses to inspect one experience, not as seven independent products or agents:

1. **Human Outcome** — what becomes easier, clearer, faster, safer, or more possible.
2. **Information & Hierarchy** — what the brain notices first, second, and never has to hunt for.
3. **Interaction & Journey** — the shortest confident route from intent to completion, including recovery.
4. **Visual & Identity** — deliberate product character rather than model-default aesthetics.
5. **Motion, Rhythm & Emotion** — pacing and transitions that reinforce meaning, continuity, attention, and feeling.
6. **System, Responsive & Accessibility** — behavior across real devices, content, states, abilities, and reduced-motion needs.
7. **Trust, Proof & Sovereignty** — verification, transparency, undo/recovery, ownership, and continued human control.

Converge the lenses into one coherent design before Build.

## Design decision hierarchy

When values conflict, optimize in this order unless an approved product-specific contract proves another order is required:

1. user outcome;
2. clarity;
3. trust and safety;
4. accessibility;
5. ease and speed;
6. human control and recoverability;
7. coherence and consistency;
8. emotional resonance and delight;
9. visual polish;
10. novelty.

A lower priority cannot silently defeat a higher one.

## Anti-default rule

For every material design choice ask:

**What exists because the product deliberately chose it, and what exists because the model defaulted to it?**

A default may survive only when it is the best justified choice for this product.

Do not copy a reference's identity. Extract the quality mechanism: hierarchy, density, rhythm, restraint, interaction model, proof, clarity, or another observable property.

## Progressive capability

Advanced capability should appear where context makes it useful. Do not make the primary workflow permanently compete with secondary or expert features.

A feature may exist without occupying the primary interface.

## Evidence

Taste is not reduced to one metric, but design claims still require evidence appropriate to the claim:

- usability behavior;
- task completion;
- accessibility checks;
- responsive/device evidence;
- reference comparison;
- actual user feedback;
- interaction recordings or screenshots;
- performance/runtime behavior;
- trust/recovery behavior;
- independent critique.

Confidence is not evidence.

## Stopping rule

Do not stop because the artifact looks polished or because a builder is tired of iterating.

Stop subtraction when the critic can name the protected quality that the next removal would damage.

That protected quality must connect to the approved Intent → Standard → Evidence contract.

## Client Zero

Vibe Engineering applies this standard to itself before teaching or distributing it as a client standard.
